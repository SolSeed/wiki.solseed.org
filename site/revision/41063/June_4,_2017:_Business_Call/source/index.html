== Revisions to We Believe and the outcomes we focus on ==

=== What if there's no future for us? ===
* Ben: I’m currently in one of my periods of believing there is no way to save civilization or even have recovery from collapse be possible.  So what fruits can we offer to ourselves and others that aren’t dependent on whether we individually and as a culture have a future that is longer than a decade or two?  And so Eric’s comment (Can we sell the fruit of not wasting your free time on entertainment?) is an interesting step toward “How can you feel a life with no future is not wasted?” “Where does the meaning come from?”
* Eric: The meaning can be very clearly seen through metaphorium telescopes. Life isn’t just precious because it is going to survive, Life is precious every moment that it is surviving (like mathematical integration).  Every moment you are alive is precious for all time.  Every moment that any creature has been alive has been precious from the beginning of time and will be precious until the end of time.  Because Life is not a difference of kind from non-Life but a difference of degree from non-Life, the universe has been somewhat alive from the beginning (not very) and will be somewhat alive (hopefully a lot) "until the end."  Each moment that every creature is alive is held precious by the entire universe.
** Brandon: I hold every moment that every creature is alive to be precious in this moment. Therefore the whole universe, through me, is holding it precious.
*** If it's about what's required for your survival strategy, each of our survival strategies for parts of the universe is part of the universe's survival strategy
** Eric: Even the degree of preciousness held by a rock is nonzero. 
** Brandon: I don't think this metaphysics is what we're selling up front.
*** Eric: I think we should put it out there even though it won't appeal to very many people, but it doesn't have to be the main thing
* Ben: even if we don’t say the bit about no future, we still need the thing to sell about how to feel life is worthy now.
** Brandon: Buddhist practices of mindfulness, universal compassion, connection … the Western Buddhist Teachings/Practices
** Eric: Activism in very general terms, i.e. taking action toward a better future, is also worth selling
*** Brandon: You can do this even if you know your efforts will be in vain.
*** Eric: Some people say that Buddhism lets you detach from all outcomes to the point where you don't have to be the change you want to see in the world, but that's completely false.
**** Brandon: But it isn't about vigorously campaigning against things
** Eric: If you take the principles of mindfulness and presence then of course the “being in this moment” matters even if you don’t ultimately succeed and that you know that you are not going to succeed
*** Ben: How can you choose a tactic if not by figuring out what's the most likely to achieve the outcome?
**** Eric: Expressions of compassion
*** Brandon: the canonical example; the Buddhist priest knows that the gun that is coming up to their head is going to kill them and yet they hold compassion for every creature in the universe including that which is about kill them.  The monk does not despair because they are not attached to the outcome but instead to being the compassionate.
**** Ben: I will never be able to reach that extreme of nonattachment
**** Brandon: Does your despair/suffering integrate positively? If not, why are you unwilling to give it up?
**** Ben: Not unwilling but unable. I don't see how you can love a thing without caring whether it lives or dies in the near future.
*** Brandon: I often suffer pointlessly/in a non-noble way, and then suffer over the fact that I'm doing it … I have at times not suffered under the same circumstances, and it was better
*** Eric: likewise … not suffering is a fruit of mindfulness
