First Ad Experiment

Keyword: Carl Sagan

Ad text: Carl Sagan liked religion the magnificence of the Universe as revealed by modern science

Landing page text: SolSeed ... Embrace Science. Cherish the Earth. Bring Life! The SolSeed Movement - A Community of Practice

The SolSeed movement is a religion which believes that

  • science tells the truth, and
  • life, all life, is precious and
  • the purpose of humanity is to nurture life.

Science has revealed a story about a universe billions of years old which may last forever. This fills us with awe.

We revere the 600 million years of history of complex life on Earth and we are filled with hope by the youth of life when compared to the billions of years of future it will enjoy. We see its story as a story of exuberantly bursting through boundaries to flower and spread.

Humanity is part of this story and we believe that we have a key role to play. The probes that we have sent to explore our solar system have sent back picture after picture of barren lifeless worlds. For every life-rich world like Earth there may be billions of lifeless worlds in the universe.

This creates in us an urge to do two things:

  • nurture and care for the precious life here on Earth and
  • help life burst through the final boundary and take root and flower amongst the stars; to help the Earth give birth to a family of living worlds.

Bring Life!

Morality

  • Ben: at Sol 2013 Brandon talked about how holding people accountable for bad actions is a form of otherizing and that there are more effective means of shaping people's behaviors ... and about how we should see things as "good but could be better" rather than "wrong and needs to change"
  • So what does Brandon mean by the word "wrong?"
  • Non-dualistic perspective (key to Buddhist enlightenment): "It's all good" ... no morality
  • Dualistic perspective: "Right and wrong, us and them"
  • Both perspectives have failings, so one of our opportunities is to switch to the appropriate perspective for each situation we encounter
  • Six Thinking Hats (Edward de Bono) is about the same idea (adopting different perspectives and avoiding getting stuck in any one perspective ... but from a functional "group process" perspective rather than a metaphysical/spiritual perspective)
  • Positive (Yellow), Negative (Black), and Emotional evaluation (Red), Just the facts (White), Brainstorming (Green), Process (Blue)
  • Brandon: This is why I can say things that seem paradoxical and contradictory, because things are paradoxical and contradictory
  • Ben is worried that when Brandon talks this way, people who don't know him well will write him off as a crackpot
  • Brandon: Some people aren't ready for this type of message
  • Ben: More concerned about people who will actively try and harm us, if we not only say that we are some new "scientific religion" but also that we sometimes don't think in terms of right and wrong
  • Brandon: I don't think we need to worry about people physically attacking us over morality
  • Ben: You're right that people basically don't do that in our current cultural environment, but it's been common in the past, is still common in many parts of the world, and could become common again here as a result of some of the massive changes that characterize this period of history
  • Brandon: Part of the arete of humanity is working out what is the better or worse thing to do, but a lot of bad things happen when people take their concepts of right and wrong too seriously
  • Ben: The glib answer is "our morality is different, we want to do what's best for all life" ... but then again, what happens when the interests of two living things are pitted against each other?
  • Brandon: Under our morality, being animals (who must consume other beings to survive) is fraught with moral difficulty
