== Discussion ==
* Do we want to grow a lot, or are we okay with staying this size?
** Ben: I'd like to get some more people, not sure how many
** Brandon: I'd like to grow the number of "tithing" members and members doing [[wisdom]] practice, but not "at any cost"
*** Not sure whether "tithing" has to be done on a percentage-of-income basis

* Engines of sustainable growth (from ''The Lean Start-Up'' book)
** Paid: Ads aren't sustainable on their own; we need a compounding effect (e.g. raising money from the new people)
*** Ben doesn't like this model; it's too close to what L. Ron Hubbard did
*** Brandon agrees that if it were successful, we could well optimize just for getting new members to give us money so we can buy more eyeballs
** Viral: SolSeed members could spread SolSeed as part of their regular practice
*** Examples: Sending someone money using PayPal requires that the recipient use PayPal; Hotmail grew quickly early on by advertising itself in every email message users sent
*** We've been ''trying'' to do this with our [[topical community protocols]], but not effectively since we often don't include talking about SolSeed as part of our protocols
** '''Sticky''': Ensuring members are deeply engaged for long periods of time
*** This is a good fit for what we've been doing
*** Requires that attrition is lower than the rate of new members arriving
*** How to get people in? This may include ads, but we're not optimizing for large numbers of people coming in
** Word-of-mouth: Having it be natural for people to share their love of your group or product with friends
*** Doesn't really work for religions in our society

* Why read books on start-ups rather than religious or social movements?
** Brandon suspects that the latter wouldn't be focused enough on practical frameworks for growth

* Can we measure our impact?
** The impact of people's donations to life-bringing causes is hard to measure
** Self-reporting may be good enough for wisdom practice
** Need to do this so we can perform valid experiments to see what works
*** Example: Spend 5 cents per click on AdWords, trying to achieve some measurable goal
**** Hypothesis: If we find the right people, we won't need a long funnel (since existing active members didn't take long to become deeply engaged
**** Hypothesis 2: There are enough people who want SolSeed that we can grow to a movement of thousands
**** Hypothesis 2.1: We can discover and reach the right people using AdWords (meaning they are reachable through the internet)
**** Definition (for purposes of the experiment): "The right people" are the ones who will spend hours engaging on the website
*** Example: Short-term measurable goal to bring life to people through [[CleanDrinkingWater]]
**** Fits with Ben's concept of success as people living their values and ideals by building things
**** Goal: Try to raise $100,000 from members of SolSeed for clean drinking water
**** Assumptions:
***** There are enough people, they have enough money and are willing to spend it, and this will remain true until they've spent the total (regardless of how quickly they do so)
***** SolSeed members will feel good about living their values in a mediated way (through giving money rather than digging wells)
** Try testing things on "cohorts" of a hundred people at a time
*** First cohort: everyone we've touched in the process of getting to 4 active members
*** Find out what creates value for members
**** Why don't the work bee calls create value for Shelley?
**** Hypothesis: Work bees, services, and seasonal celebrations are "sticky" (they keep people coming back)
