This was a session at the August 2018 Resilient Communities Mini-Unconference.

Participants

  • Brandon (convener)
  • Ben (note-taker)
  • Shawn
  • Eric
  • Judy
  • Steve
  • Shelley (briefly)

Discussion

  • Judy: Should UBI be provided only to people who really need it?
  • Perhaps on an honor system? I want to have faith in people
  • Shawn: The idea is similar to the negative income tax for the poor
  • Brandon: I've listened to one podcast on UBI, with Sam Harris interviewing presidential candidate Andrew Yang
  • Side note: Harris is a New Atheist, but more spiritual than most
  • Yang has run companies and been a professor at Harvard Business School (where the students are motivated by just wanting to make lots of money)
  • Yang also supports social currency that aligns purchasing power with work that isn't currently paid, e.g. caring for the elderly
  • Everyone starts with an equal amount of "esteem" points and hands them out to each other as reward for work they appreciate
  • Related to time banking
  • The context of Yang's UBI proposal is that automation is rapidly replacing jobs, and there may be no replacement jobs coming
  • Yang proposes a UBI of $1000 per month, which is below the poverty line, and would be mutually exclusive with other assistance programs for the poor
  • At one point he and Harris express the motivation of "not wanting to have to buy bulletproof cars for their children" due to crime arising from immense income inequality
  • Steve (much later): I don't see a problem with a world where robots do all the hard labor
  • Shawn: Tell that to people whose family identities are wrapped up in that labor, and who want to feel valued for their contributions
  • Judy: But if you had UBI, it would give you time and resources to figure out another way to contribute to society
  • Issues with UBI
  • Shawn: "Rentier states" are using something like UBI to buy off their own citizens, e.g. with oil money, making the government unaccountable to those citizens
  • Judy: I thought I saw an article in Yes Magazine saying UBI would be somehow destructive to the Black community
  • Shawn: How do we pay for it?
  • Judy: Get the wealthy and corporations to pay
  • Brandon: Yang wants to use a European Union-style value-added tax
  • Steve and Shawn: That would be paid by consumers, which seems to defeat the purpose
  • Brandon: Trades in the economy add value; this tax and UBI distribution means more money moving around and therefore adds the value needed to make it worthwhile
  • Argument about perpetual growth and whether or not it's motivated by compound-interest loans
  • Brandon: The three origins of value are:
  • Using up stored value (e.g. oil)
  • Accelerating the movement of money (not counting banks' financial tricks and automated trading algorithms)
  • New ideas and inventions
  • Steve: e.g. if we find a more valuable use for rhodium, the world supply of rhodium suddenly has more value without having to mine any more
  • Shawn: But you pay a resource cost for every invention, generally underpinned by cheap fossil energy
  • Argument about replacing fossil fuels with renewables
  • Brandon: I support UBI because it makes me feel like things would be possible that weren't before, e.g. for new college graduates who don't currently see a positive future for themselves
  • Shawn: I see UBI as life support for the current system, when what we need is system change
  • More argument about compound interest and "unpayable" debt
  • Eric and Judy: In Canada, they have rules so that banks can't lend more money than the borrower can pay back
  • Steve: Lenin viciously attacked reformers as enemies of revolution
  • Ben: What can the SolSeed Movement do about any of this huge national economic stuff?
  • Brandon: Support UBI
