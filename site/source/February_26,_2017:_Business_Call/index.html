### Tactics: Growth ideas that are relevant to the times we live in
Eric
- I feel that the work we are doing is relevant to the times we live in. Creating a vision that is beautiful instead of greedy is as relevant as you can get.
  - The biggest problem with what happened in the States is that people started to just live with visions of fear and greediness and self-protection and feelings of not being safe.
  - If we keep pushing our vision that is hopeful and beautiful, we're fighting in the best way we can.
- There might be short-term things that are more important in the short term.  But we are a long-term group and somebody needs to stay on the long-term.
  - Ben: How do we know what kind of long term we are going to have?
  - Eric: We don't, but we offer a vision of the long term.

Brandon:
- We're *strategically* a long-term organization, but our tactics are shaped by what's happening in the moment, and this is a great opportunity for us because people are awake in a way that they're often not.
  - They can see that narrative matters and that we're not living a good story. We can help offer a better story that they can live and share.
- The assault against science and the Earth, and to a lesser degree the assault against Social Justice, are opportunities for us to speak about. Unitarian Universalists are better placed to focus on Social Justice.
  - Ben: The Social Justice direction we can use is that we value diversity above all else.  The main reason that we value Life is because it creates diversity.  
  - Eric: So we need to link diversity of life in general to diversity of humanity.
  - Brandon: But diversity of life is much more about embracing science and cherishing the Earth than about human politics.

Ben:
- I’m hesitant to propose *Empirical World* as a SolSeed tactic because it really wouldn’t be talking about space hardly at all. Does it make sense for us to choose tactics that don’t focus on our unique aspect of [The Destiny](/The_Destiny) and Life spreading beyond Earth?
  - Brandon: There is no SolSeed without embracing science. Our whole story is science-driven, and one of our most defining elements is that we seek to build a religious narrative in which science is powerful. This feels like work that is consistent with SolSeed.
- The other reason I’m hesitant is that for *Empirical World* to succeed on a large scale, it needs to avoid being associated with entities that are not reputable (e.g., tiny new religions).
  - Brandon: Consider the *Christian Science Monitor.* Christian Science itself is crazy, but that publication is great, and people trust it.
- Ben: I’m not sure I want to put a lot of energy into this because it seems to have very low probability of success.
  - Brandon: We could do a pilot and see what the response is.
  - Ben: The issue I'd want to cover first is democracy. How bad is the problem of democracy in decline? What are the main components of the problem and the major causes? Who is working to solve the problem and what methods are having the most success?
    - We can count how many people aren't allowed to vote, and how many people can vote but choose not to.
    - Brandon: The confidence various generations have in the democratic process.
    - Brandon: Start with a question and go wherever it takes to answer that question, even if the answer doesn't match our hypothesis. If it doesn't, that actually gives the show more integrity.
