### Discussion
#### Parts 1 and 2, at the Sanders' place on Friday morning and the nearby wetland on Saturday morning
- Brandon: We're rewarded pretty well from living in the mainstream ... it's hard to give that up
- Eric and Michelle can easily pursue different careers and ideologies while living together ... much harder to do in a community of, say, 10 people
  - Ben: [Windward](/Windward) has a partial solution: work 4 hours farming in the community, 4 hours in moneymaking activities that are likely very diverse
- The Aranmore Homeowners' Association pays for a shared pool where families meet, so it's kind of an intentional community (IC), but way too expensive to be tribal
  - From *Beyond Civilization* by Daniel Quinn: Tribal means people are all there for the same work driven by the same values
  - In *Parable of the Talents*, Acorn's work is to become a viable community and gain strength
- Brandon: To me, IC means IC for its own sake, and I don't want that
  - Ben: But so many ICs are about trying to live the New Story, acting as a research lab for ways of relating to each other
  - Brandon: That's not us
  - Ben: But we need some of it to live more closely together than apartment dwellers
  - Eric: Project dorms, like the forestry dorm where Michelle lived for three summers, may be a good model ... not community, just living in a conventional hierarchy
- Brandon hates the idea of people arguing over rules and whether or not people are following them
  - Eric: Use Holacracy ... for example, have one "kitchen boss" role with a responsibility to make sure everyone cleans up after using the kitchen
    - Bring in hierarchy as a failure mode ... in Holacracy, a manager only makes a decision as a last resort
    - The rest of the time, managers just predict and remove blocking issues
  - Ben: But I don't want to use business metaphors for the part of community that's just about how to live!

#### Part 3, at Tryon on Saturday afternoon
- Eric: It really seems like we aren't serious ... we started this event after the [Shaping Change Conference](/Shaping_Change_Conference), wanting to do something inspired by [Octavia Butler](/Octavia_Butler), but that's not enough of a motivator
- Brandon: We could easily get deep into building a community, and I'm leery of that ... things to try in life are a scarce resource
  - We're also voting with our feet: the Saumurs, the Sanders, and Ben all moved homes this year, but none of us moved toward each other
- Eric: I've kind of committed to doing *something* with Buckleberry ... I want to watch the trees there grow
- Shelley: Our lives are full and community processes like consensus are time-costly ... I like the things I currently spend time on
  - Eric: Maybe we could limit the time cost to ourselves by starting with a resort model, where "all is taken care of" by paid employees, then evolve toward people who live there doing those services?
  - Ted: Where I lived in Chicago, there was low overhead due to small size ... also no common space, we'd just visit each other ... we had process but never went deep into it in the 5 to 6 years we were there ... there were no big conflicts
    - Ben: Sounds like you were very lucky
    - Ted: We had single men and couples only ... a baby was born and the family moved out due to a job
    - We wanted to be a bike community ... it was a vision of social change and a shared struggle (like Acorn's struggle to survive)
- Eric: I've been interested in IC since college, but saw how groups like SCA and the Green Party spent so much time arguing
  - I studied the Dobe !Kung in Botswana ... their answer to unresolvable conflict was to require both disputants to move to other camps ... maybe we can do that once we've built a network of communities
- The value of things being finished and neat (see also [EcoVillageTour#General things we learned](/EcoVillageTour#General_things_we_learned))
  - Mother Earth School has a big problem with kids being around Tryon's unsafe unfinished projects
  - Artists and creatives generally won't have this value
- Can ecovillages attract people who find the work of community easy? Wouldn't those people already have non-intentional communities?
